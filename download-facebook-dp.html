Hi, I'm Romit Kumar Yadav

A self-taught web developer and builder from Bihar, India. I make websites and small online tools, and I learn most of what I know by building something and putting it online to see what happens.

My Story

I finished Class 12 in the Science stream with PCM and Biology. That's not the usual start for someone who ends up building websites, and I didn't follow the usual path afterward either. I never had a perfect plan. What I have is a self-directed path where I pick something, try to build it, and figure out the rest as I go.

I started with the basics: HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Then I kept adding to the pile, including Android with Kotlin, Firebase, Cloudflare Workers, GitHub and working with APIs. None of it came from one course or one roadmap. It came from wanting to make a thing work and not knowing how yet.

The biggest change came when I stopped building only for practice and started publishing real websites. A website that only runs on your laptop teaches you very little. A website that real people can find — or can't find — teaches you a lot, very quickly. That's when I started caring less about just writing code and more about the whole product: who it's for, how people find it, and whether it actually helps them.

What I Build

I like simple websites and online tools that solve one specific problem. I'm drawn to anything that's complicated, repetitive or just annoying for users, and I enjoy making it simpler.

Right now I'm most interested in:

  • Web tools and consumer-focused websites
  • AI-powered products
  • Automation
  • Developer tools
  • Android apps
  • Digital products and online businesses

My Projects

I've built and experimented with several sites and tools, including:

  • FBviddl — A free online Facebook video, reel, image and DP downloader.
  • ThreadsGrab — A downloader for Threads content.
  • ReditGrab — A downloader for Reddit content.

I'm not going to pretend these are huge successes. They're small projects. Their real value for me is what they taught me about how the internet and online products work in practice, which you can't really learn from a tutorial.

What I've Learned

Running real websites taught me things I never would have learned just by writing code:

  • Search and SEO: how search intent works, why indexing matters, and what technical SEO actually involves
  • Performance and user experience: page speed, and why a site that feels slow or confusing loses people quickly
  • Deployment and infrastructure: putting sites live, working with Cloudflare, and managing domains
  • Analytics: looking at real numbers instead of guessing
  • Monetization and advertising: how online products try to earn money, and how hard that is
  • Troubleshooting: something is always broken, and finding out why is most of the job
  • Building for actual users: people don't use things the way you imagined

How I Learn

I learn by building. I've never been great at only watching tutorials. I'd rather take an idea, build a rough first version, publish it, and see what happens. Then I find the problems, research the fixes, and improve it.

My cycle looks like this:

Learn → Build → Publish → Measure → Improve → Repeat

Some projects work a little. Some don't work at all. Both teach me something, and I'm comfortable with that.

Technology & AI

Here's what I've worked with so far: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Android/Kotlin, Firebase, Cloudflare Workers, GitHub, APIs, and website deployment, plus SEO, performance and analytics.

I also use AI a lot. It helps me learn new things, research problems, debug, experiment and build faster. I see it as a tool — like a very fast study partner — but I'm not an AI expert and I don't claim to be. I still have to understand what I'm building, and I still make the decisions.

Beyond Coding

I'm not only interested in code. I like YouTube, storytelling, content creation and personal branding, and I want to build an audience online at some point. Creating things — whether that's a website or a video — is what really interests me.

Outside of that, I enjoy football, gaming and movies, and I like trying out new ideas just to see if they work.

What I'm Working Toward

My long-term goal is to build useful technology products, websites and, eventually, technology businesses that can reach users beyond India.

I haven't achieved that yet. Today I'm working on small projects, improving my technical skills and learning from the ones that fail. I have limits: limited formal experience, limited resources, and plenty of areas I still don't understand well. But I want the small projects I'm building now to lead to something much bigger.

My Philosophy

Build something small, publish it, and learn from what happens. Failing at something real teaches me more than reading about it. I try to stay curious, stay practical, and keep improving.

How to download your own Facebook photos and videos

Back up media from your own Facebook account with Download Your Information, choose useful date ranges, and keep the archive stored safely.

Home › Blog › How to download your own Facebook photos and videos

25 October 2026 · FBviddl guide

If you want a copy of many photos, videos, posts, or account details from your own Facebook profile, an account archive is usually the right place to start. A single-post downloader and Facebook’s Download Your Information tool solve different problems: one handles an accessible individual link; the other is Facebook’s route for requesting a copy of your account data.

Choose the method for the job

Use Facebook’s account tools when you are backing up your own material in bulk or need a record tied to your profile. Use a single-link tool only when you need one publicly accessible post and have permission to save it. A third-party page should not ask you for your Facebook password to fetch a public link.

Facebook’s information and download tools let you review or request categories of account information. The exact settings may vary by account and interface version.

Request an account archive

  1. Sign in to Facebook directly through its official website or app.
  2. Open Settings and look for Accounts Center or the section named Your information and permissions.
  3. Choose the option to download your information. Facebook may let you select a profile, categories, a date range, and a file format.
  4. Select only the information you need when the options allow it. A smaller date range or fewer categories can make the request easier to organize.
  5. Submit the request, then return to Facebook’s settings to check when the archive is ready.

Menu labels can change. If the steps on your screen differ, follow Facebook’s current in-product instructions rather than entering your login details on a third-party site.

Organize the archive before you rely on it

After downloading, keep the original archive intact and make a separate working copy before sorting files. Use folders based on year or event, and preserve any included index files that help connect media to posts or dates. If the archive contains personal information, store it in a location protected by your device’s normal security controls.

Open a few files from different dates to confirm that the download completed and the archive contains the expected material. A downloaded archive can be large, so make sure the destination has enough free storage before starting.

When you only need one public post

For a single public Reel, copy its direct link and check that it opens while you are signed out of Facebook. Then use the matching Reel downloader. For a photo in a post or an album, use the image downloader. These tools do not replace an account archive and cannot retrieve private posts, deleted content, or material that Facebook does not make available to the request.

Protect your personal copy

An archive may contain more than the media you intended to keep. Do not upload it to an unknown service or share the full archive when you only mean to send one photo. Extract or copy only the files you need, and remove extra personal details before sharing.

Quick answers

Can I use a public-post downloader to export my whole profile?

No. A link-based downloader handles individual supported links. Use Facebook’s own information-download settings for an account archive.

Should I give a downloader my Facebook password?

No. A tool that handles a public link should not need your account password. Sign in only on Facebook’s official app or website.
